Featured research — M.Sc. thesis

Enumeration of tree diversity in the urban landscape of Yamuna Nagar city, Haryana. Forest Research Institute, 2025.

57tree species recorded
27families identified
~60%native by species count
Research
Surveyed tree diversity across Yamunanagar's urban zones — roads, parks, industrial areas — using QGIS and Google Earth Pro for spatial delineation and mapping, with field-based species identification.
Finding
Native species made up roughly 60% of documented diversity — but counting actual planted trees told a different story: cities keep choosing fast-growing exotics over native species, despite documented ecological costs. Diversity on paper is not diversity on the ground.
Action
Developed zone-wise recommendations for more ecologically appropriate, resilient urban planting, submitted directly to inform city-level greening decisions.

Evidence: full thesis document in the documents folder below.

Sustainability reporting — ONGC internship

Research internship, ONGC Dehradun (Dec 2023 – Feb 2024), under Dr. Sanjay Bhutani. Compared how Indian oil and gas companies report their sustainability performance against the UN's 17 SDGs.

The claims spanned all 17 goals; the substance concentrated in far fewer. My own recommendations called for what was consistently missing — quantified, formalized impact commitments rather than broad ones. It's the same question my thesis asks of cities, asked of an industry instead: does the paperwork match the ground reality?

This research was presented as a poster and won 2nd position at the International Young Researcher's Conclave (IYRC) 2024, UPES Dehradun.

Evidence: ONGC training completion certificate and conference poster in the documents folder below.

Leadership & community

Across my B.Sc. and M.Sc., leadership has usually meant being the person who turns a research question into something a group can act on. I served as Head of my Hostel Committee, a class representative, and a Placement Cell member — coordinating students, organising activities, and working through disagreements that don't resolve themselves. I carried that into project leadership too, as presenter lead and project team lead for my team's strategy at the IGNFA national hackathon.

That pattern of leading a project through to something usable runs across my academic work more broadly: alongside my M.Sc. thesis and the ONGC sustainability research, my graduation dissertation examined indigenous weather-prediction knowledge and its role in agricultural planning across districts of Himachal Pradesh, and a village attachment programme had me lead a socio-economic survey of rural households — landholding patterns, cropping systems, livelihood strategies — to understand resource dependency at the community level. Different subjects, same habit: research that has to end in something someone else can use.

What I'm building toward

Proposed / future direction — not yet implemented.

Yamunanagar is where I have the data, not where the question ends. My thesis found that cities plant fast-growing exotics over native species even when the ecological cost is documented — and I have no way to know, alone, whether that's a Haryana habit or a pattern repeating in fast-urbanizing cities everywhere. That's the real project: a youth-led, evidence-based urban greening model — data-driven mapping, native-species planting, student and community participation instead of top-down planning — built to be tested and adapted city to city, not filed away as one thesis about one place.

FAS is where I want to start that test. With a network of Global Change Ambassadors working across 80-plus countries, I want to compare Yamunanagar's pattern against what youth are already seeing in their own cities, adapt what doesn't transfer, and build toward a framework other student foresters can pick up anywhere — not just in India. Becoming a Global Change Ambassador is how that comparison stays alive after the summit ends: I'd bring the method back to train the next cohort of student foresters here, while staying in the network long enough to find out if the gap I found is local, national, or something every fast-growing city on the planet is quietly getting wrong.
